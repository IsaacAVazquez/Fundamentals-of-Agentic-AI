# Why This Course Exists

## The AI Landscape Right Now

There is **massive uncertainty** in AI right now.

- New tools launch every week
- Models leapfrog each other every month
- The tool you mastered last quarter might be obsolete next quarter

> So what should you actually learn?

## Four Levels of AI Fluency

## Level 1 Is Not Enough

- **Level 1:** You can code, you can prompt, you use Copilot and ChatGPT
- There are **millions** of people here already
- Knowing how to use the latest tool is not a differentiator
- The tool you mastered last month might not exist next year

**Warning**

Tool fluency alone will not make you stand out.

## Level 4 Is Not Required

**Note**

You do **not** need a PhD in AI. You do not need to build an LLM from scratch. That is Level 4 — a very different career path.

Most people in the industry don’t operate at Level 4. And most never need to.

## The Goal: Level 2

**Important**

This course gives you **fluency at Level 2** — a conceptual understanding of how AI actually works, so you can reason about it, evaluate it, and build with it, regardless of which tools survive.

**Understand AI as a concept — not just manage the latest tool.**

## The Roles Are Merging

Before AI: Product → Design → Engineering was a **waterfall**. Three separate jobs, three separate teams.

With AI: the boundaries are **dissolving**. One person can now do what used to take a full team.

[Source: Anthropic — *Product Management on the AI Exponential*](https://claude.com/blog/product-management-on-the-ai-exponential)

# How This Class Came to Be

## It Started as a Student Initiative

- Nobody assigned this course. It began as a **student initiative** — a proposal, not a mandate.
- The reason was simple: **the market is moving faster than any curriculum can follow**.
- Job posts stopped asking for people who *use* AI tools, and started asking for people who can **reason about AI systems** — scope them, evaluate them, and know when to say no.
- There was no class that took someone from “what is a variable” to “I built and evaluated an agent.” So I wrote one.

# How This Course Works

## It’s Technical, But Not Complicated

- This is a **technical** course
- But the knowledge isn’t super complicated
- I honestly believe you can grasp everything I know in **seven classes**

> “Being a software developer is just being a glorified Google searcher.”

And I mean that. The barrier to understanding this material is much lower than you think.

## The Seven-Class Arc

One journey, seven stops — each class builds on the last:

1.  **Code & Programming Foundations** — read, change, and test real code
2.  **Software Systems** — frontend to database to deployment, shipped live
3.  **Machine Learning Foundations** — how models learn from data
4.  **Deep Learning & Transformers** — from neurons to attention to LLMs
5.  **LLM Behavior, Prompting & Retrieval** — steer models and ground them in your knowledge
6.  **Production LLMs & Single Agents** — turn a useful prompt into a reliable product
7.  **Multi-Agent Systems, Ops & Safety** — bounded agent teams you can trust

**Tip**

The finale is a two-class **capstone** (Assignment 5): a multi-agent insight pipeline that turns ~10,000 messy feedback records into a business decision you can audit. Class 5’s Knowledge Hub keeps growing too — optional v2 and v3 builds for anyone who wants more.

## The AI Policy

**Important**

**AI use is not merely permitted in this course — it is required.** Every assignment is designed to be built with an AI coding agent. Refusing to use AI will make this course harder, not more honest.

What is required alongside it:

- **You must understand and be able to explain everything you submit** — every line, every file, every schema decision. If you cannot explain it, you did not do the assignment; you watched someone else do it.
- **Review the diff before you accept it.**
- **Cite nothing, disclose everything** — no footnoting AI usage, but be honest if asked what you wrote versus what an agent wrote.
- **Never paste confidential or personal data into a model.**

The honor-code check is simple: I may ask you to **explain any part of your submission, verbally, live**. The tools can write the code — they cannot sit in the meeting for you.
